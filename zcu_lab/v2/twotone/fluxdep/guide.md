**Readers:** operator, agent. Map the qubit transition versus DC bias and decide whether the map supports a flux calibration.

## What Run measures

At each DC bias, Run steps the RF frequency of the qubit probe. Each shot applies the optional reset, plays the probe, reads out the resonator, then waits for relaxation. A drive near a qubit transition changes the averaged resonator IQ. Trace a continuous ridge across the bias sweep to see how the qubit transition moves. The map is spectral contrast, not excited-state probability.

## Set up and run

1. Bind the target qubit and its resonator. Open the experiment, or press **Defaults** to rebuild the form from current bindings. **Refresh** updates choices without discarding edits.
2. Select **Flux Device**, the DC source to step, and its **Flux unit**. `A` and `V` are amperes and volts; `native` leaves the device's own coordinate unchanged. Run does not convert units. Review the start, stop and count under **DC bias**. The starting range covers the predicted half- and integer-flux positions on the selected source, padded by 10% at each end, with 101 points. It uses the prior flux calibration and the other bias values captured with the form. Missing calibration leaves endpoints blank.
3. Fill **Probe RF frequency** start and stop in MHz around the expected line. Open and Defaults leave the RF endpoints blank and start with 1001 points. The qubit probe template takes its channel from the bound qubit; the sweep supplies its RF frequencies. The probe gain starts at 0.1 a.u.; check that it reveals the line without broadening it enough to hide nearby transitions. The waveform length sets the drive duration. The RF mixer field, if used by the pulse, is its separate LO frequency, not a replacement for the RF sweep.
4. Use a calibrated **Readout**. Open and Defaults prefer the bound resonator's readout slot. Without it, they seed a pulse from the resonator RF frequency, generator, ADC and time of flight. Check pulse length and the independent ADC window and trigger offset. Choose **Reset** only if you intend to prepare a specific starting state before each probe; blank omits reset.
5. Check **Reps** (shots per RF point per round), **Rounds** (averaging passes at each bias), **Relax delay** (wait after each shot, in µs), and **Acquisition retries** (how many attempts may be made after a transient failure at one bias). The form starts with 1000 reps, 10 rounds, 1 µs and zero retries.

## Read the figures

The **Run** map uses DC bias on the horizontal axis and probe RF frequency on the vertical axis. In each bias row, subtract the median complex IQ of that spectrum and take the magnitude. The color scale shows this contrast in a.u. Blank cells have no data. The row counter gives the biases with data, including a row that is still averaging. The adjacent plot compares latest and previous bias spectra on the same contrast scale; it helps distinguish a moving ridge from a single bright cell.

The **Analyze** picker offers a separately processed view to place the red half-flux and blue integer-flux lines. Its frequency axis is in GHz (1 GHz = 1000 MHz). Dark features in the top map are stronger smoothed contrast, normalized per spectrum in the phase-aware view. Mirror loss compares the two sides of a candidate symmetry line. Lower loss means a more symmetric local map, not proof of the physical assignment. Click near a line, move it and click to commit. Use **Conjugate Line** to inspect the other line's symmetry, **Swap Lines** to exchange labels and **Auto Align** only to refine a candidate already near the correct turning point. Click **Done** to record the selections. A valid line separation is not a quality test for the calibration.

The saved **flux-line picks** figure uses the same projection, color scale and MHz frequency as the final Run map. The two vertical lines show the recorded biases, not additional measured spectra or a fit. The figure reports the selected positions and twice their absolute separation as the candidate period. The Analyze acquisition summary gives the experiment, run ID and saved Reps × Rounds. This is a two-dimensional map that locates a spectral ridge; it does not show per-point shot error bars.

## Decide whether to apply the result

Use the candidate period only if you can track the same transition through both adjacent sweet spots. The line should turn and look locally symmetric around each selection. The prior flux frame or a separate calibration distinguishes half from integer flux; symmetry alone does not. If a turning point is absent or outside the map, do not apply the period or offset. Extend **DC bias** toward the missing sweet spot using the last validated calibration to estimate its position. Widen the RF endpoints if the ridge reaches a frequency edge. For isolated or noisy bright cells, first verify the probe and readout at a single bias, then increase averaging or take a finer grid before trusting a ridge. Do not infer a different qubit frequency from a single cell.

For adjacent half/integer picks at 0.10 mA and 4.00 mA, the period candidate is 7.80 mA. Review its sign separately; writeback keeps the prior sign, or uses a positive sign if none was set. With signed period `P_s`, the half-flux position `x_half` gives `offset = 0.5 − x_half/P_s − Σ x_o/P_o`, where the sum uses the other calibrated DC lines in the Run-start snapshot. The preview lists those contributions. The period, offset and record are separate proposals. **Done** does not write them; accept only supported proposals. Save retains the map and analysis. Older files without a usable cfg can still show the recorded map and picks, but cannot label a historical bias unit or propose a calibration without the captured inputs.

## MCP recipe

Use `twotone_flux_dep` to set **Probe RF frequency** from `center_mhz ± span_mhz/2` and to set its count with `freq_points`. Use `flux_device`, `flux_unit`, `flux_range` and `flux_points` for **Flux Device**, **Flux unit** and the **DC bias** sweep. The unit must match the cached device; the recipe does not connect it. `gain` sets **Probe Pulse › Gain** in a.u.; `readout_ref` selects **Readout**. Apply any additional form changes with `overrides` before Run. The interactive Analyze records **Half flux**, **Integer flux** and **Flux period** (`flux_half`, `flux_integer`, `flux_period`) in the selected device units. The recipe asks before applying candidates and saves after a successful Run, including when no analysis is available.
